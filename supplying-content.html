Arbortech – Content We Need From You

A few parts of the store are built and waiting on words, images or a sign-off that only Arbortech can provide. This page lists each one: what we need, what format, and where it ends up — so you can send it through in one batch, or enter it yourself with the manual open.

1. How this works

Everything on this page follows the same pattern. For each item you can either:

  • Send it to us — put the content in a shared document (Word or Google Doc is fine) and email it through. Label every piece with the product name or page it belongs to, and the audience or country if it applies to one. We'll load it in and switch on anything that needs switching on.
  • Enter it yourself — each item below links to the section of the CMS Manual that walks through the exact screen. If you enter content yourself, drop us a line afterwards so we can flip any display switches and check it renders everywhere it should.

Plain text travels best

When sending copy, plain unformatted text beats heavily-styled Word documents — the store applies its own styling. Bold, italics, links and lists are all fine to indicate; ornate fonts, colours and text boxes don't survive the trip.

2. Category page intros

Every shop category page (Woodworking, Masonry & Trade, and so on) has space for a short introduction under its heading. The three main audience landing pages already have theirs — the copy you suggested in your July review is live under each hero image, and it's yours to reword any time. The remaining category pages have no intro yet; a sentence or two per page sets the scene for shoppers and helps Google understand the page.

What to write (for the pages still without one)

  • 20–50 words per page, plain text, one short paragraph. No headings, no images.
  • Write for the audience of that page: the Woodworking pages speak to hobbyists and carvers; Masonry & Trade and Landscaping speak to professional tradespeople.
  • Say what the range is and who it's for — not a slogan. Think "the first thing a helpful staff member would say". The three live intros are good models.

3. Product copy by audience & market

The same product can read differently depending on who's looking (a woodworker vs a masonry tradesperson — see the PDP Guide §6) and where they are (Australia, US, Canada, UK, Germany — PDP Guide §7). Your July review asked for exactly this — per-audience image, description, bundles, video and features — and the plumbing for all of it is built; what's missing is the words. None of it is compulsory: anything without audience- or market-specific copy simply shows the product's default content.

4. Videos & images

Two media slots on the product page are waiting on Arbortech assets. Both are optional per product — nothing looks broken without them — but they lift the products that have them.

Overview video

  • Per product: a video link (a YouTube link is fine), an optional poster image (the still shown before play), and a short title.
  • A product can also carry a different video per audience or per country — same format, just say which one it's for.

Product & audience imagery

  • New product photos: supply original files, named descriptively (allsaw-ba200x-turf-kit-1.jpg, not IMG_0421.jpg) — full sizing and naming guidance is in Manual §6.
  • If a product should lead with different photos for different audiences (e.g. masonry shots first for tradespeople), list the image order per audience and we'll wire it up.
  • One specific from your July review: the Turf Blade Kit pictures should show the two different blade types. The product photos are yours to update any time (Manual §2.1, Media) — or send the shots through and we'll load them.

Event images & links

The events calendar currently holds three placeholder events that all share the events-page banner image and have nowhere to click through to. For each real event, supply an image and a link (registration page, venue, or news post). Events are edited directly in admin under Content → Metaobjects → Event — each event also has a region setting (so a German trade show only shows on the German store) and an end date for multi-day events.

5. German translations sign-off

The German store (/de-de) now has a first-pass German translation across products, collections, pages, articles and menus. That first pass is machine-assisted: it reads well enough to test with, but it is not your voice until a German speaker has been over it. Translations are contractually Arbortech's responsibility — what we need from you is review and sign-off, and anything you change goes live immediately.

  • To review: open Apps → Translate & Adapt, pick German, and work through the resource list — each field shows English on the left, German on the right. Manual §10 covers the tool in detail. We'll also send a review worklist (a spreadsheet of every machine-translated entry) so you can tick items off rather than hunt for them.
  • To correct: edit the German column directly and Save — it goes live immediately.
  • To sign off: reply to us listing what you've reviewed and approved, so we can mark it done and stop chasing it.
  • If your translators need access: Manual §11.3 has a ready-made recipe for a translation-only login that can't touch anything else.

The legal pages are deliberately NOT machine-translated

Impressum, AGB, Datenschutz, Widerruf, Versand & Zahlung, and the terms, cookies and returns pages are legal compliance surfaces — we have left them untouched rather than put draft legal wording live. Their German text must come from Arbortech, approved by whoever owns your legal compliance. The withdrawal (Widerruf) email and form wording specifically is on the agenda for our next 1:1 session — the form works today, but no customer email can go out until that approved text lands.
